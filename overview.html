Every birding app tells you where. This one tells you when.

Pick a species, a hotspot, and a day in the coming week. It returns the probability you would actually see that bird, hour by hour — the weather forecast applied to what birders have historically reported at that spot.

One

The idea the whole thing rests on

eBird lets a birder mark a checklist complete — a declaration that they listed every species they could identify. On a complete checklist, a species that does not appear is evidence the bird was not detected. It is not missing data. It is a verified zero.

That is what makes a probability computable. A pile of sightings gives you a numerator and nothing else. Complete checklists give you the denominator: out of 412 complete lists submitted at Frick Park in week 31, how many contained a Wood Thrush? Now you can divide.

Every design decision downstream follows from protecting that zero. Only complete checklists enter the fit. The app's own “I listed everything” box is a claim recorded honestly, never a required checkbox — a gate on the save button does not produce complete checklists, it produces people ticking a box to get past it. And a bird picked from photographs is flagged uncertain, because a wrong species also makes every absence around it wrong.

Local only AI_certificate

From a sighting to a number on a phone

01

Scrape

eBird API 2.0, two calls per checklist — one for the day's feed, one for each checklist's contents. There is no bulk endpoint. Scraping deliberately lags seven days: submissions trail the outing, and scraping fresh captures only the people who log in the field.

02

Store

A DuckDB file that is never committed. Both a laptop and a CI runner write to it, so syncing merges by natural key rather than overwriting — running it twice adds nothing.

03

Split — the licensing boundary

eBird records must not be redistributed in raw form. A fitted coefficient is derived work: a number describing a pattern, not a copy of anybody's checklist. So the two go to different places.

04

Fit

One ridge logistic regression per species, refitted monthly. New checklists arrive continuously; how detection responds to hour, effort and weather does not move week to week.

05

Serve

The fitted model is a lookup table plus a sum, so the app needs no Python and no server. Roughly 200 lines of TypeScript score it in the browser, against a weather forecast fetched at request time.

Private dataset

raw eBird checklists

Licensed, not redistributable. Reached only by the pipeline, with a write token held in CI secrets.

Public dataset

fitted coefficients only

Numbers, no records. Fetched straight from a CDN — which means the browser needs no credentials at all. No token in the front-end bundle, no proxy server, no auth layer in front of the model.

What the model estimates

P(reported | hour, week, site, effort, weather) — one ridge logistic regression per species.

Effort
duration · distance · observers · travelling How hard you looked. The largest effect in the data — and not optional. Rain makes birders cut a walk short and makes birds quieter. Leave effort out and the rain coefficient silently absorbs both, so you would publish “rain lowers your chances” when half of that is “rain shortened your walk”.
Time of day
one term per hour, 06:00–20:00 Dawn chorus and the midday lull. Strongly species-dependent — generic “go early” advice underdelivers.
Season
one term per week of the year Migration, breeding song, winter absence. Indexed by day-of-year rather than date, so last August's checklists inform this August.
Site
one term per hotspot Habitat, compressed into a single number. Ridge shrinkage is what makes this possible: a site visited 89 times keeps its own estimate, one visited four times is pulled toward the county average.
Weather
wind · temperature · precipitation · cloud Kept as a separable block, because it is the thing actually under test.

Ridge rather than ordinary logistic regression because the fit otherwise separates — a site where the bird was seen on all four of its visits gets an infinite coefficient, and roughly half the species were lost that way once site effects were added. The cost is inference: ridge coefficients are biased on purpose, so there are no valid p-values here. Acceptable, because the app needs point predictions rather than significance tests.

The gap between those two is the evidence that site effects carry real information. Testing only by site would have hidden it completely, since a site the model has never seen cannot benefit from site effects by construction.

Calibration matters more here than AUC. AUC asks whether the ranking is right; a calibration error of 0.026 says that when the app tells you 40%, the true rate is close to 40%. A ranking that is never honest about magnitude is little use to someone deciding whether to get up at five.

Stated limits

  • Detection, not presence. This predicts whether you would report a bird, not whether one is there. For a birder that is the right target. It is not the same quantity, and the distinction matters to anyone reading these numbers ecologically.
  • One county — an arithmetic constraint, not a preference. Building a denominator costs two API calls per checklist: about 4,650 for a month of one county, roughly 438,000 for a year of Pennsylvania, over a million for a month of the United States.
  • Effort is endogenous. Birders extend a good outing, so duration is partly a consequence of success rather than only a cause. The effort coefficient is inflated by an unknown amount.
  • Weather sits on a 25 km grid, which attenuates its coefficients toward zero. The wind effect is probably understated.
  • Observers self-select on weather. Nobody birds in a storm, so the observed weather range is truncated and whoever does go out is unusual.
  • The eBird feed truncates any county-day above 200 checklists, under-sampling exactly the busiest spring mornings.

Not yet built

  • The leaderboard. Described in the brief, and display names are already collected for it. Nothing renders it.
  • Photo upload. Deliberately deferred — storing images is easy, reviewing them is not, and an unreviewed upload path is a moderation problem waiting.
  • An unkept promise. The sign-in page tells users their checklists improve the forecast. Nothing currently carries app-logged sightings back into the pipeline. Either build the path or change the copy — leaving both is the worst option.
